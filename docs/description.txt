Economic Analysis of Germany and Neighboring Countries

The dataset provides a comprehensive snapshot of key economic indicators for Germany and its neighboring countries, making it an excellent resource for study, especially for someone like me with a financial technology background interested in analyzing economic data.

Firstly, GDP (Gross Domestic Product) is a fundamental measure of a country's economic performance, reflecting the total value of all goods and services produced within its borders. By examining GDP figures in current and constant prices, you can gauge each country's overall economic health and growth trajectory over time. The inclusion of GDP per capita allows for a more nuanced understanding of economic development and living standards among the populations.

Inflation, as measured by the annual change in the Consumer Price Index (CPI), is a critical macroeconomic indicator that reflects the rate at which prices for goods and services are rising or falling. Understanding inflation dynamics is crucial for making informed decisions in financial markets, as it directly impacts interest rates, investment strategies, and purchasing power.

The breakdown of gross value added by sector—agriculture, industry, and services—provides insights into the composition of each country's economy. Analyzing the share of GDP contributed by each sector reveals the relative importance of agriculture, manufacturing, and services industries in driving economic growth and employment opportunities. This breakdown can also offer valuable insights into sectoral trends, structural changes, and comparative advantages among countries.

Overall, this dataset offers a rich source of information for studying and analyzing economic trends, making it a compelling choice for someone interested in leveraging data-driven approaches to understand and navigate the complexities of the global economy.

Dataset Overview

The dataset provides a holistic snapshot of essential economic metrics, including Gross Domestic Product (GDP) in current and constant prices, GDP per capita, inflation rates measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI), and the breakdown of gross value added by sector (agriculture, industry, services). These indicators offer insights into the economic health, growth trajectory, and sectoral composition of each country, facilitating a nuanced understanding of regional economic dynamics.

Explorative Data Questions
1. How has the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) of neighboring countries evolved over the years, and what are the comparative growth trends?

* Understanding the GDP trends of Germany and its neighboring countries is crucial for various reasons. First, it provides insights into the economic performance and development trajectories of neighboring nations, which can impact regional stability and cooperation. Second, it helps businesses assess market opportunities and potential risks in neighboring economies. By analyzing the GDP trends over time and comparing the growth rates among neighboring countries, we can identify patterns, potential correlations, and divergences in economic performance. This analysis aids in making informed decisions related to trade, investment, and economic policy formulation.

Method: A multi-line chart with years (2012–2024) on the x-axis and GDP in current prices (US$ bn) on the y-axis, with one differently colored line per country. This immediately reveals each country's absolute economic trajectory and allows visual comparison of growth slopes. Since Germany dominates in scale, a secondary small-multiple panel using the same line chart but with a country-specific y-axis scale would additionally highlight relative growth shape without Germany visually flattening the others.

2. What is the distribution of GDP per capita among the countries?

- This question aims to compare the distribution of GDP per capita across different countries. Analyzing the distribution can reveal disparities in income levels and living standards among these countries.

Method: A box plot (one box per country) with GDP per capita values across all years (2012–2024) forming the distribution. This shows the median, spread, and any outlier years per country at a glance. Luxembourg will stand out dramatically as a high outlier. Complementing this with a dot strip plot overlaid on the boxes where each dot is one year keeps the actual data points visible and avoids hiding the decade-long trend within the summary statistics.

---

3. How do the GDP growth rates vary across the years for each country?

- This question aims to explore the variability of GDP growth rates over the years for each country. Analyzing GDP growth rates can provide insights into the economic performance and stability of each country.

Method: A heatmap with countries on the y-axis and years (2012–2024) on the x-axis, where each cell is color-coded by the real GDP growth rate (annual change in constant prices) using a diverging color scale from red for contraction to green for expansion. This instantly exposes the 2020 COVID shock as a dark-red column across all countries while also revealing which countries had consistently strong or weak years throughout the decade, all in a single compact view.

---

4. Is there any correlation between inflation rates and GDP growth rates?

- This question aims to investigate the relationship between inflation rates and GDP growth rates across the countries. Analyzing the correlation can help understand how changes in inflation affect economic growth.

Method: A scatter plot with inflation rate (CPI annual change) on the x-axis and real GDP growth rate on the y-axis, where each dot represents one country-year combination (9 countries × 10 years = 90 data points), color-coded by country. A linear regression trend line with a confidence band is overlaid to visually test the direction and strength of the relationship. Labeling notable outliers (e.g., France 2020 at −8% growth, Switzerland's negative inflation years) adds analytical depth to the correlation story.

5. How does the contribution of agriculture, industry, and services to the gross value added vary across different countries over the years?

- This question aims to explore the varying contributions of agriculture, industry, and services to the gross value added (GVA) across different countries over time. By investigating these sectoral dynamics, we gain insights into how each sector influences the economy. Agriculture, industry, and services represent distinct pillars of economic activity, with agriculture typically reflecting primary sector activities, industry encompassing manufacturing and production, and services embodying a diverse range of service-based activities. Analyzing the shifts in sectoral contributions to GVA allows us to understand economic diversification, structural transformation, and overall economic performance. Such insights inform policy decisions, investment strategies, and economic development initiatives, facilitating cross-country comparisons and collaborative efforts toward sustainable growth.

Method: A faceted 100% stacked bar chart one panel per country, with years on the x-axis and percentage share on the y-axis, using consistent colors across panels for agriculture, industry, and services. The 100% stacking makes the relative proportions comparable both within a country over time and across countries. This makes it straightforward to spot whether a country's service sector is expanding, whether industry is declining, and how structurally different Poland and Czechia (high industry shares) look compared to Luxembourg or France (high services shares).

6. Which countries were most economically resilient during the COVID-19 shock of 2020, and how quickly did they recover in 2021?
The year 2020 is a clear structural break visible in the data (with GDP growth rates plunging across the board). Identifying which economies absorbed the shock best and rebounded fastest reveals differences in economic robustness and policy effectiveness.

Method: A grouped bar chart with countries on the x-axis and three grouped bars per country representing GDP growth rates for 2019 (pre-shock), 2020 (shock year), and 2021 (recovery). Color-coded by year, this makes it easy to compare the depth of each country's contraction and the speed of its rebound side-by-side.

7.  Has the GDP per capita ranking among the countries remained stable or shifted significantly over the 2012–2024 period?

While absolute GDP values show size, the relative ordering of countries by living standard (GDP per capita) reveals whether the economic hierarchy is stable or whether any countries are catching up or falling behind.

Method: A bump chart (ranking chart) with years on the x-axis (2012–2024) and rank position on the y-axis (1 = highest GDP per capita). Each country is represented by a differently colored line. Crossings in the lines indicate rank changes and make convergence or divergence immediately visible.
-------
8. How does the industry-to-services ratio differ across countries, and has there been a measurable trend of deindustrialization (declining industry share, rising services share) over the decade?

Structural economic shifts away from industry toward services is a hallmark of advanced economies. Examining whether all countries show this trend equally — or whether some (like Poland and Czechia with high industry shares) are still industrializing give a nuanced information.

Method: A stacked area chart per country, with years on the x-axis and percentage share on the y-axis, with three colored bands for agriculture, industry, and services. This makes it easy to spot whether the services band is expanding and the industry band contracting over time. A complementary slope chart comparing industry share in 2012 vs. 2024 for all countries in one view would sharpen the cross-country comparison.
